Practice room

Get grilled here, not there.

The questions that kill rounds are rarely the ones on your rehearsal list. Run a simulated partner meeting and find out which answer you don't have — while it still costs you nothing.

  • Investor archetypes — operator, metrics-first, thesis-driven, contrarian.
  • Questions drawn from your own weak spots, not a generic bank.
  • Scored transcript with the specific sentences that lost the room.
  • Term-sheet rehearsal — negotiate a liquidation preference before it's real.

A typical journey

What eighteen months looks like.

Week 1

Baseline

First report. Score of 61, Tier C. Three material risks named: concentration, CAC payback, and a TAM slide that won't survive contact.

Week 2–8

Remediation

Work the top three milestones. Restate market sizing bottom-up. Pricing tier introduced to shorten payback. Deck rewritten section by section with the mentor.

Week 9

Re-run

Score moves to 71. Market dimension goes from the weakest to the second strongest. Two risks downgraded, one closed.

Week 10–20

Proof

Two design partners land in a second vertical. Concentration falls below 30%. Mock meetings run weekly against different archetypes.

Week 21

Match

Score at 78, Tier B+. Investor shortlist generated: 23 funds ranked by thesis fit. Warm-path analysis identifies eleven routes in.

Week 22+

Raise

Conversations start with the objections already answered. The readiness snapshot goes out with the deck instead of after the third call.

Monthly is typical, or whenever something material changes — a big customer, a key hire, a pricing change, a new competitor round. The score recalculates continuously from connected data; a full agent re-run is what refreshes the memo and the risk ranking.
